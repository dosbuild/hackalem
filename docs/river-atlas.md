# River atlas / Есильский атлас

Alem’s identity starts with the Esil river, real city volumes and the small human
choices made around them. The two worlds share an atlas-like drawing language:
mineral color, graphite structure and quiet paper. Modern facades and historical
felt retain their own materials and silhouettes.

Geometry and lighting carry the scene. Pencil marks belong to shadows, surface
variation and selected contour edges; they stay attached to world or object
coordinates as the camera moves. They must clarify depth without obscuring a face,
window, doorway or readable label. Flat color keeps geographic identities legible.

Paper supports reading and choosing. Clear body type, fixed numerical columns,
measured text wrapping and exposed primary actions take priority over texture.
Noto Serif headings give the atlas its editorial character. Noto Sans body text
preserves Cyrillic, Kazakh coverage and readable calculations; small Shantell time
notes supply the handwritten accent. Local licenses ship with the game.

The renderer uses procedural materials, shared geometry and bounded render targets.
No downloaded reference image or raster texture is used to imitate another game.
The 140 catalogue pictures come from the actual original project meshes with the
same material pass and camera. Aya’s original atlas and the code-native city mark
retain their authoring provenance.

Keep spatial continuity between the resident, proposal, construction and result.
The player should recognize the same place and person when returning. Every color,
mark and transition must remain stable during repeated play, world switching,
reduced motion and camera input. Visual animation does not change the calculation.

This direction records the intended original identity. Rendered desktop/phone
inspection and the current [release evidence](release.md) establish its acceptance.
