# Khanate: fictional world and material sources

Khanate's settlement, people, coordinates and civic institutions are fictional.
It is a visual interpretation of the same educational scenario as Astana, not a
reconstruction of eighteenth-century Astana.

Collapsible timber lattices, felt coverings, roof poles and crowns, rope bindings
and woven bands inform the yurt models. Material and assembly references include
[UNESCO's yurt documentation](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-knowledge-and-skills-in-making-kyrgyz-kazakh-and-karakalpak-yurts-turkic-nomadic-dwellings-02284)
and its [assembly photographs](https://ich.unesco.org/en/10-representative-list-00748?id=00998&include=slideshow_inc.php).

The [National Central Museum's collections](https://csmrk.kz/en/collections)
inform felt furnishings, reed mats, woven bags, decorated metal and leather vessels.
Many surviving collection objects are later than the fictional setting; they are
material references, not evidence of particular buildings or residents.

All geometry, icons and ornaments were created for Alem. No museum or UNESCO
photographs, scans or models are redistributed. Paired curves and woven bands are
original geometric interpretations rather than tracings of collection objects.
Warm felt, pale paper, sage vegetation, a turquoise river, clay, brass and indigo
form the palette. Physical structures and residents share the world camera,
lighting and scale; construction remains an illustration of a game decision.

The world's images use these native models. Fonts and geographic source data keep
their separate licenses; see [sources and licenses](../ATTRIBUTION.md).
